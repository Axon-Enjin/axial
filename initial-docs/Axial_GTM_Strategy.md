# Go-To-Market (GTM) Strategy: Axial

## Strategic Overview
To ensure Axial achieves rapid market penetration and proves its core value proposition, the GTM strategy targets businesses that experience the exact structural friction Axial solves: the agonizing gap between strict B2B payment terms and relentless bi-weekly operational liabilities.

---

## 1. Primary Target Market: B2B Tech, Creative, and Specialized Manpower Agencies
These are modern, service-based businesses that rely heavily on human capital. They are the perfect "wedge" into the market because they are digitally native, structurally lean, and highly susceptible to cash flow choke points.

### Ideal Customer Profile (ICP):
* **Size:** Small to Medium (10 to 50 employees).
* **Revenue Model:** Project-based retainers, milestone billing, or custom software/digital infrastructure contracts. 
* **The Client Base:** Larger, established corporate clients (e.g., traditional enterprises, legacy brands upgrading their digital presence) who rigidly enforce Net 60 or Net 90 payment terms.

### The Specific Pain Point:
Their primary expense is talent. Developers, designers, and project managers cannot wait 60 days to get paid. Agency founders constantly stress over bridging the gap between a signed corporate contract and the actual cash arriving to fund their bi-weekly payroll and statutory (SSS, PhilHealth, Pag-IBIG) counterpart contributions.

### The "Wedge" Advantage (Why Them?):
* As tech-forward teams (like custom software agencies), they will instantly appreciate Axial's minimalist, glassmorphic UI and "unrushed" digital experience. 
* They are highly networked. Founders frequently interact in third-wave coffee shops and networking events, creating organic, word-of-mouth referral loops for the product.
* They easily grasp the value of API bridges and automated logic, significantly shortening the sales cycle.

---

## 2. Secondary Target Market: Institutional F&B Suppliers & Niche B2B Distributors
Once the infrastructure is battle-tested with service agencies, the next expansion phase targets businesses dealing with physical supply chains, where the liquidity crunch is exacerbated by inventory costs.

### Ideal Customer Profile (ICP):
* **Size:** Medium enterprises scaling up their production capacity.
* **Revenue Model:** Bulk volume sales, recurring institutional purchase orders.
* **The Client Base:** Supermarket chains, corporate cafeterias, hotel groups, or large restaurant franchises holding supplier payments for 90 to 120 days.

### The Specific Pain Point:
To fulfill a new, massive purchase order, they need to buy raw materials *today*. However, their working capital is entirely locked up in unpaid invoices from the previous month’s delivery. Furthermore, they often employ a mix of regular staff and contractual labor, turning BIR and statutory payroll reporting into a chaotic, error-prone nightmare of manual spreadsheets.

### The Expansion Advantage:
* Invoice volumes are significantly higher and highly predictable, providing excellent, stable yields for the liquidity providers (lenders) on the other side of the Stellar smart contracts.
* Winning over these suppliers integrates Axial directly into the lifeblood of local commerce, proving the system can handle the rigorous, high-frequency demands of physical goods distribution and complex tax regulatory logic.

---

## 3. The GTM Phasing & Rollout Strategy
1. **Phase 1 (The Wedge):** Launch Axial by onboarding custom software and creative agencies first. They will act as highly-engaged beta testers who can provide excellent UX feedback and appreciate the modern architecture.
2. **Phase 2 (Validation & Refinement):** Ensure atomic swaps, BIR EIS oracle bridging, and statutory splitting prove perfectly seamless in a high-skill service environment. Leverage founder networks for organic growth.
3. **Phase 3 (Expansion):** Pivot marketing and direct sales to capture institutional F&B suppliers and B2B distributors, scaling up the liquidity volume flowing through the Stellar network.
